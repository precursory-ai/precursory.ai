And it weighs the market, capacity, and constraints as multiple scenarios at once, fast enough to send the quote to the shipper and lock the rate with a third-party carrier before the picture has had time to shift.

The moment the pickup delay hit, Precursory modeled the cascade — not just to this delivery, but to the load already planned after it. It surfaced the fix to dispatch: hold the second seat-swap until after delivery, skip the fuel break since there was enough fuel to reach Joliet, all within HOS limits — then kept watching to confirm the next load stayed protected too.

HOW NUCLEUS CLOSES THE GAP

Nucleus plays forward the scenarios of this coordination game and catches the delay while there's still a window to act. It connects the signals that today sit scattered — telematics, appointment windows, customer SLA terms, traffic, and weather — so a delay's downstream impact is visible the moment it starts, not after it's spread. It reasons across planners, fleet managers, and customer success together — routing efficiency, the driver relationship, the customer relationship — instead of any one function's goal alone, so the response reflects the full consequence. And it weighs every affected load as a parallel scenario in real time, fast enough that there's still a real choice to make: reroute, adjust, or notify the customer, before anything is actually late.

// INTERVENTION RECOMMENDED
Redirect load: ID → UT · Day 4
Reposition 6 trailers: WA → ID · power-only · 3rd-party carrier

Idaho read as balanced through day two — nothing looked wrong from the top down. But as new loads kept tendering against thinning capacity, the region started draining, a trend no single planner watching their own loads would have caught in time. By day four, Precursory had already flagged it and recommended the fix: redirect a load to a neighboring lane, and reposition trailers in before the gap became a missed pickup.

HOW NUCLEUS CLOSES THE GAP

Nucleus connects truck and trailer location, driver availability, equipment health, and incoming load signals into one continuously updated view. It reasons for the whole network's balance, not any one load or planner's immediate fill, so today's assignment doesn't strand tomorrow's equipment. And it re-solves the assignment as a rolling set of scenarios in real time, adjusting as loads, drivers, and equipment shift, instead of drifting out of balance between planning cycles.
